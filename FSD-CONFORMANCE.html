What changed in this round

Six things, four of them gaps in the spec and two found while looking.

  • Job import — a recruiter can paste a URL, paste a description, or upload a PDF or Word file, and get an editable draft back. Closes JOB-002, JOB-008, JOB-009, REC-007 and AC-003.
  • Preferred-skill gaps — the engine computed which nice-to-haves were missing and then discarded the list, so nothing downstream could tell a must-have gap from a nice-to-have one. Closes MAT-006 and GAP-001.
  • Candidate contact privacy — not in the spec. Any signed-in account could read any candidate's email address by visiting their profile URL.
  • Seat role enforcement — not in the spec. A job-seeker account could accept a recruiter invitation and become a company member while still being a candidate.

Two security bugs, neither of them a spec item

The email exposure is the one worth acting on quickly. /u/<id> required only that the viewer be signed in, so any account could walk profile IDs and collect email addresses, salary targets and "quietly open to offers" flags. Access is now limited to the candidate themselves, a recruiter they have applied to, and either party to a mutual match.

What is still open, and why

Five requirements are genuinely unbuilt, and one is a deliberate refusal.

  • AC-010, AC-011, AC-012, BR-009 — rematching on change. The feed and every explanation are recomputed from live data on each request, so a candidate who edits their skills sees different jobs immediately. What does not update is the stored score on an existing match, which was written once at the moment two people swiped. So a match made in July can still display a score from an older model. This needs a rescoring pass, and a decision from you about whether a score changing under an existing conversation is desirable.
  • APP-002 — resume version on an application. Resumes are versioned, but an application does not record which version was current when it was sent, so a candidate who later replaces their CV silently changes what a recruiter appears to have received.
  • NFR-006 — background processing. Resume parsing and job import run inside the request. Acceptable at current volume; the first thing to break under load.
  • CAN-005 — work-authorization categories. Deliberately not built. Collecting visa categories invites hiring decisions on national origin, which is what the IRCA anti-discrimination provision prohibits. The codebase substitutes a single "will you need sponsorship?" question, which is the lawful form of the same business need, and there is a CI guard that fails the build if a category ever reaches the matching path. This is a decision to keep, not a gap to close — but it is your decision, and it is worth your counsel confirming it.

The 32 partials are mostly one shape: the server is complete and tested, and nothing in the product calls it.

  • Job management (REC-003) — edit, pause, close and publish-a-draft all work through the API and have tests. No screen calls them, so the composer's "Save as draft" writes something the recruiter can never reopen. This is the largest usability hole in the product today.
  • Recruiter filters (CAND-002) — skills, experience, work model and salary all filter correctly as query parameters. The sourcing screen sends none of them.
  • Team management (ORG-004 to ORG-008) — invitations, seats, revocation and member removal are all implemented and enforced. There is no team screen.
  • Report and block (MATCH-007) — both endpoints work and are tested. Neither has a button.
  • Applicant list (APP-003) — the API returns a recruiter's applicants; nothing displays them.

Production readiness

The engineering side is in good shape. 700 automated tests pass, the build compiles, and the security, compliance and matching suites are genuinely thorough. What is not ready is mostly not code.

Blocking, and only you can do these

  • Rotate the exposed credentials. The database password, AUTH_SECRET, CRON_SECRET and the RapidAPI key have all appeared in screenshots or transcripts.
  • Make the repository private if it is not already.
  • Set CRON_SECRET — without it the nightly ingestion refuses to run in production, so no job source pulls on a schedule however many keys are configured.
  • A verified sending domain, so password resets and verification emails actually arrive.

Needed before launch, but not by me

  • An independent bias audit — Jobsy ranks candidates for employers, which makes it an Automated Employment Decision Tool under NYC Local Law 144. The audit is annual, must be independent, and its summary has to be published. The engine is built to be auditable — decomposable scores, a versioned model, no protected attributes anywhere near it — but being auditable is not the same as having been audited.
  • Counsel review of the Terms, the Privacy Policy and the AEDT notice.
  • Accessibility verification. One WCAG contrast assertion exists, on one element. Focus rings, reduced motion and pinch-zoom are implemented; nothing has been tested with a keyboard or a screen reader.
  • Load testing. Nothing in the suite measures latency under concurrency.
